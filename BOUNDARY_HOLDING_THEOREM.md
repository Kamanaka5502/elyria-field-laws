# Boundary Holding Theorem

## Core Claim

A boundary is not a line of refusal.

A boundary is the lawful surface where holding, transfer, refusal, halt, rebound, restart, or partial admission must be resolved.

Boundary behavior determines whether a basin is governing movement or merely containing pressure until failure.

---

## Formal Context

A basin holds movement only while remaining lawful capacity and standing conditions survive.

```text
Phi(x) = C(x) - M(x)
```

The lawful region of continuation is:

```text
R = { z | A(z) >= 0, Phi(z) >= 0, U(z) = 1 }
```

The boundary is reached when proposed movement approaches the edge of `R`, the edge of basin capacity, or the edge of authority-valid continuation.

---

## Theorem

If a proposed movement reaches a boundary condition, the system must resolve the boundary before consequence status is admitted.

Boundary resolution may return:

```text
ALLOW
PARTIAL
TRANSFER
REDIRECT
REFUSE
HALT
REBOUND
RESTART
```

But boundary contact alone does not authorize continuation.

```text
Boundary contact is not standing.
Boundary pressure is not permission.
Boundary breach is not authority.
```

---

## Holding Versus Failure

A basin is not proven by the absence of visible failure.

A basin is proven by invariant behavior at the boundary.

It must show what happens when:

```text
capacity reaches zero
pressure increases
burden shifts
transfer path closes
authority changes
evidence goes stale
lineage breaks
trajectory approaches breach
```

If the system cannot resolve these conditions before consequence status, it is not governing boundary behavior.

---

## Boundary Resolution Rules

### 1. Hold

If the basin can carry the proposed movement without violating capacity, authority, or invariants, movement may continue.

### 2. Transfer

If the basin cannot carry the movement locally but a lawful receiving basin exists, movement may transfer.

Transfer must be receipted.

### 3. Partial

If only a bounded fraction can be carried, the allowed fraction may partially bind and the remainder remains non-binding.

### 4. Refuse

If no lawful holding, transfer, or bounded partial path exists, movement must refuse before consequence status.

### 5. Halt

If continued movement expands illegitimate consequence, the system must halt the trajectory.

### 6. Restart

Restart is allowed only through a lawful transition that preserves invariant law.

Restart does not erase breach.

---

## Breach Rule

A breach is attempted movement against lawful structure.

Breach does not create authority.

Breach reveals the boundary.

```text
Breach -> Halt -> Judgement -> Restart
```

The restart path must be lawful.

Otherwise, the movement remains non-binding.

---

## Relation to Projection Error

Projection error occurs when a dashboard, diagram, log, or receipt shows boundary contact but does not preserve boundary resolution.

Observing a boundary is not governing a boundary.

The proof is not that pressure was seen.

The proof is that unlawful consequence did not bind when the boundary was reached.

---

## Relation to Field Laws

The kernel rule remains:

```text
BIND(M) is defined only when K(M) = ALLOW.
```

Boundary holding explains why `K(M)` may return something other than `ALLOW` even when motion is mechanically possible.

Mechanical possibility does not prove lawful continuation.

---

## Doctrine

The boundary is where the field proves itself.

```text
A basin is not proven by what it contains.
A basin is proven by what it refuses, transfers, halts, or lawfully carries at the boundary.
```
