# Basin Holding

## Core Claim

A basin is a shaped region of lawful pressure.

It determines what can remain, what must move, what must transfer, what must refuse, and what may lawfully continue.

Discovery does not begin when a possibility is named.

Discovery begins when the basin holds.

---

## Basin as Lawful Holding Region

A basin is not a container metaphor.

A basin is a lawful holding region under constraint.

It is defined by capacity, pressure, boundary, transfer aperture, changed-condition behavior, and continuation law.

A named structure becomes discovery-grade only when it holds under:

```text
constraint
repeatability
transfer
capacity
changed-condition behavior
lawful continuation
```

---

## Basin Elements

| Basin element | Formal role |
| --- | --- |
| Basin | Lawful holding region under constraint |
| Boundary | Where holding fails, transfer begins, or continuation breaks |
| Drain | Transfer aperture; not deletion |
| Capacity residual | Remaining lawful movement |
| Pressure / burden | Constraint testing whether the basin can hold |
| Proof | Invariant behavior at the boundary |

---

## Capacity Residual

Remaining lawful movement is the capacity left after carried burden, pressure, mass, or constraint is accounted for.

```text
Phi(x) = C(x) - M(x)
```

Where:

```text
Phi(x) = remaining lawful capacity
C(x) = available capacity
M(x) = carried mass, burden, pressure, or constraint
```

If `Phi(x) < 0`, the basin cannot lawfully carry the proposed movement.

The movement cannot force itself into consequence status merely because it is desired, visible, named, selected, or mechanically executable.

---

## Basin Failure

A basin fails when the proposed movement exceeds lawful holding capacity or violates boundary conditions.

Failure may resolve as:

```text
REFUSE
HALT
REDIRECT
TRANSFER
REBOUND
RESTART
PARTIAL
```

The outcome depends on whether lawful continuation remains possible.

---

## Drain Is Transfer, Not Deletion

A drain is not erasure.

A drain is a lawful transfer aperture.

When content leaves one basin, it must either enter another lawful holding region, route through a valid transfer path, or be refused before consequence status.

Untracked disposal is projection error.

It observes disappearance from one surface while failing to prove lawful transfer in the resolving field.

---

## Holding Under Changed Conditions

A basin that holds at one time-slice may fail after conditions change.

Standing must be re-evaluated when:

```text
capacity changes
burden increases
pressure shifts
authority changes
evidence goes stale
lineage breaks
transfer path closes
continuation scope narrows
```

Prior holding does not guarantee future holding.

A basin must preserve lawful continuation under current field conditions.

---

## Basin Proof Surface

A basin proof must show:

1. proposed movement;
2. basin identity;
3. capacity before movement;
4. carried burden or pressure;
5. remaining lawful capacity;
6. boundary condition;
7. transfer path if movement leaves the basin;
8. decision outcome;
9. receipt / witness object;
10. replay path proving the same capacity relation.

Without this proof surface, the basin is only named.

It has not demonstrated lawful holding.

---

## Relation to Field Laws

Basin holding strengthens the field-law rule:

```text
BIND(M) is defined only when K(M) = ALLOW.
```

The basin explains one reason `K(M)` may refuse, redirect, transfer, partially admit, or halt movement:

```text
the field cannot lawfully carry the proposed pressure.
```

---

## Doctrine

Possibility is not holding.

Naming is not discovery.

Execution is not capacity.

A basin proves itself only when it holds under pressure without losing invariant structure.

```text
Discovery begins when the basin holds.
```
