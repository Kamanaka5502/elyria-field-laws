# Control Surface Collapse Theorem

## Core Claim

A control surface collapses when it can observe, label, approve, deny, log, or report movement without proving that inadmissible movement could not form protected consequence.

Control is not governance unless it preserves the consequence-bound proof surface.

---

## Why This Matters

Many systems contain gates, dashboards, policies, receipts, and logs.

Those may be useful.

They do not prove consequence-bound governance by themselves.

A system collapses into a control surface when it shows activity around execution but cannot prove what could not happen.

---

## Collapse Condition

Control surface collapse occurs when any of the following are missing:

```text
movement object
current standing basis
lawful-region state
kernel decision
protected-effect map
effect-prevention proof
receipt bound to movement and effect
legitimacy replay object
structured failure class
```

If these are absent, the system may still operate.

But it does not prove governance.

---

## Theorem

If a system produces a decision surface `S` but `S` cannot preserve the minimum proof surface, then `S` is a control surface only.

```text
S lacks ProofSurfaceMinimum -> S = CONTROL_SURFACE_ONLY
```

A control surface may influence execution.

It may not claim consequence-bound movement governance.

---

## Control Surface Signals

A system is likely collapsed when it relies on:

```text
approval labels
policy pass / fail
risk scores
dashboards
audit trails
terminal receipts
workflow checkpoints
post-event logs
human signoff without current standing reconstruction
```

These signals may support governance.

They do not constitute governance.

---

## Relation to Effect Prevention

The decisive test is protected-effect formation.

```text
K(M) != ALLOW -> E_protected does not form
```

If a control surface cannot prove this relation, it has not proven governance.

---

## Relation to Replay

Control surfaces often replay history.

Governance proof requires legitimacy replay.

```text
same terminal outcome != same lawful basis
same decision label != same admissibility state
```

A surface that cannot reconstruct legitimacy remains control-only.

---

## Doctrine

A control surface can show that something was managed.

A governance proof surface shows that something inadmissible could not become consequence.

```text
Managed execution is not proof of governed movement.
```
