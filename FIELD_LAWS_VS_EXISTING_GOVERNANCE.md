# Elyria Field Laws vs Existing Governance Surfaces

This comparison is intended to place the category accurately without reducing adjacent systems or claiming they are unnecessary.

## Category distinction

Existing governance systems often control access, policy evaluation, workflow progression, approval state, auditability, or post-execution monitoring.

Elyria Field Laws resolves a different question:

> May this proposed movement acquire binding consequence under current authority, evidence, lineage, standing, trajectory, and witness conditions?

## Non-replacement position

Elyria Field Laws is not positioned as a replacement for:

- identity and access management
- RBAC or ABAC
- policy engines
- workflow orchestrators
- approval systems
- audit platforms
- monitoring systems
- agent tool routers

Those systems may form intent, provide evidence, constrain interfaces, or preserve operational history.

Elyria Field Laws governs the consequence boundary.

## Buyer interpretation

The value proposition is not that existing controls are useless.

The value proposition is that controls can remain present while invalid movement still becomes real.

The proof surface therefore asks:

```text
What protected effect was prevented?
Why was binding undefined?
What evidence supported the decision?
Can legitimacy be reconstructed later?
```
